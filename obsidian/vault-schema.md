# Wiki article style

- Open with why the concept matters, then build from intuition to formal detail.
- Scaffold step by step: order material so each step depends only on what came
  before it, name the step it represents (e.g. "Setting Up the Objective" before
  "Deriving the Gradient"), and never introduce a term or result before its
  prerequisite has been established.
- Prefer a minimal, source-supported example before generalization.
- Preserve definitions, equations, assumptions, limitations, and worked reasoning exactly.
- Explain relationships and contrasts in prose with inline `[[wikilinks]]`.
- Never invent facts, examples, citations, or relationships absent from the source notes.
- Be concise by default: state each point once, without filler transitions or
  padding. Spend extra sentences only where they earn their place — a genuinely
  clarifying analogy, or an explanation for a step that doesn't reduce further
  without losing understanding. Never spend them restating a point already made.
